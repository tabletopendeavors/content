Miniature Preperation and Painting

X-ACTO Knife: Nothing is more synonymous with cutting things than an X-ACTO knife, sometimes called a hobby knife or scalpel. If you need small, precise cuts, this is what you reach for. It is also surprisingly good at cleaning mold lines, and replacement blades can be had for relatively cheap.

RUITOOL Model Nippers: I used to swear by my Citadel Fine Detail Snippers, but they don't make that particular product anymore. These are an affordable single-edged set, similar to the Tamiya God Hand everyone raves about.

Army Painter Miniature and Model Drill: This is a good kit for drilling into plastic and other small or soft objects, and allows for plenty of control. Just make sure you create a pilot hole. Be aware can only accept bits up to 3mm in diameter, so if you need something larger you'll have to look elsewhere.

Army Painter Drill Bit Set: The Army Painter Miniature and Model Drill comes with some bits, but notably is missing 2mm and 3mm. This set gives you everything you need. Above 3mm and it might be time to consider another solution.

Army Painter Model Files: While I have been using Citadel’s files up to this point, the Army Painter Model Files are just a better value, and provide for a better diversity of applications. All that said, there is nothing stoppng you from using any small file you might find at a hardware store, just make sure they have a fine grit.

Army Painter Tweezers: Tweezers are one of those tools you don't think about until suddenly you need them. You get a fine point and a flat point with this set, very helpful for assembly fiddly kits. Be aware these are metal, so they aren't great for dealing with magnets.

Magnets

In WYSIWYG (What You See is What You Get) style games like Warhammer 40K, magnets are a must if you don’t want to lock yourself down. I find 2mm, 3mm, and 5mm discs will cover 99% of your needs. I prefer 1mm thickness where possible, but 2mm won’t be an issue for most applications.

I am providing links to products I have personally purchased, but when it comes to magnets, it really doesn't matter what brand you use, only the dimensions.

2mm x 1mm Disc Magnets: This is your go-to if you want to magnetize infantry models. Packs typically contain hundreds of magnets, but you'd be surprised how quickly you make run out if you magnetize hoard armies.

3mm x 1mm Disc Magnets: Despite only a 50% increase in diameter than the 2x1mm, these 3x1mm Disc Magnets are much stronger and able to handle much larger jobs then their smaller siblings. Think vehicle size weapons, mechanical limbs, and more.

5mm x 2mm Disc Magnets: When in doubt, bust out the 5x2mm Disc Magnets. These will handle any job where magnets are appropriate. For instance, I used them to attach my Astra Militarum Sentinel’s torso to it’s legs, and these aren’t about to let go. If 5x2mm isn’t big enough for what you’re trying to do, you can jump up to these 10x2mm Disc Magnets, but it might be time to consider if other methods, such as pinning, would be more appropriate.

8mm x 2mm Disc Magnets: We're getting unwieldy now. Consider these special purpose, and consider pairing anything heavy enough to warrant magnets this big with pinning.

Terrain Tools and Materials

Woodland Scenics FS639 Static King: I regret waiting so long to invest in a static grass applicator. Sure, there are cheaper options than the Static King, but you’re going to struggle to find an applicator as convenient and competent as this one. It’s loaded with nice-to-haves that I think make it worth the investment if you’re going to be working with large volumes of static grass. If you only plan on the occasional application, maybe a fine-meshed sieve will do the trick.

General Equipment

Proxxon Hot Wire Cutter: This hot wire cutter is easy to setup and use, and allows for quick and precise replication of measured cuts.

Vortex Mixer: This has been a game changer in my painting workflow. Add a stainless steel ball bearing to your paint pot, give it a go on the mixer, and in no time even the roughest paints will be properly mixed.

3D Printing

My personal experience with 3D printing is limited to a single model of resin printer, but as my knowledge of this niche of the hobby grows, so will this section. Thankfully, 3D printing is easier than ever, and at a place where a casual hobbyist can succeed.

ELEGOO Mars 3: I have a whole video on this one. It’s incredibly easy to get running and the print quality is great for most applications. Ideal for miniatures and other prints that won’t be put under any meaningful mechanical strain. Elegoo customer support has also been great to me, with replacement parts (even those not listed on their web store) being easy to come by.

ELEGOO Mercury X Cure and Wash Bundle: This is a package of convenience. The washer doesn’t do anything that can’t be done with a small tub and a toothbrush, and the agitator can’t get every nook you need it to, but it does make the process of cleaning your prints easier. The UV Curer, on the other hand, feels like a must have. Everything I’ve put through this has come out evenly cured, and the shielding keeps those nasty UV rays at bay.

ELEGOO Water Washable 3D Printer Resin: I haven’t compared this to standard resin, but with how convenient it is I might just never be bothered to. Not having to use Isopropyl Alcohol to clean models simplifies a great deal of the cleanup process, and as far as I can tell, you should expect comparable detail and rigidness from this resin.

Workspace

Everyone needs a comfortable place to work. The items in this category are about making the place you do your hobbying more constructive for you.

Fiskars 24" x 36" Self Healing Rotary Cutting Mat: It’s far cheaper to replace a mat than a desk, table, or counter. These mats are great for protecting your work surface, and the printed grid and guide lines are a great plus.

Neatfi XL Task Lamp: You ahve to be able to sea what you’re working on, and this lamp (or something like it) is all you need. It features multiple intensity levels and diffuses the light over a wide area to provide softer light to you’re work area, and the arm provides plenty of articulation.

Paper Towel Holder: I like to keep paper towels nearby for unexpected messes, and this lets me mount it under the work surface where it’s quick to access without taking valuable space. You don’t need this one specifically, but you should consider picking up this workspace improvement.

Video Production and Streaming

I’m working hard to make sure every one of my videos is better than the last. While there are many things to improve on, some of that improvement comes from investing in equipment. For those of you curious as to what I use while filming or streaming, here’s the list.

Shure MVL Lavalier Microphone: Talking head shots are no fun with a big ol’ microphone clogging up the shot, which is why I invested in this Shure MVL Lavalier Microphone. I tried cheaper options before settling on this, finding the MVL to be the most affordable lavalier microphone with a noise floor that I found acceptable.

Shure SM7B Vocal Microphone: I'll start by saying this is overkill for youtube. The Shure SM7B is a beast, and it sucks up energy like one as well. This is the microphone I use for voice overs and it is overqualified for the job. If I only did videos, I wouldn’t have spent the money on this, but I also dabble in music, and this thing delivers. Just make sure the interface you have is up to the task of powering this.

Focusrite Scarlett 2i2: I love my Focusrite Scarlett 2i2, but I want to caution that it doesn’t have the gain for something like the Shure SM7B (though the 4th gen seems to fix this), which I also have (See the Cloudlifter below). That said, it is still a great interface that can drive most XLR microphones you would actually want to use, and works great with any DAW software, of which I personally use Reaper. I use it not just for videos, but to record guitar and vocals. The Focusrite Scarlett Solo may prove a better alternative for those not so interested in the music side of things, or even the Elgato Wave XLR, which is designed for streamers and drives 75 dB of gain, enough for even the aforementioned SM7B.

Cloud Microphones Cloudlifter CL-1: I have the Cloudlifter out of necessity. The Shure SM7B is a hungry mic, hungrier than many small interfaces can handle. The cloudlifter converts phantom power (48v) into extra gain for the SM7B. That said, it’s pricey, and for most people it makes more sense to just get an interface with higher gain to begin with.

Elgato Key Light: Elgato Key Light: This is my primary light when filming. It provides a good amount of light at 2800 Lumens, good diffusion, is built like a tank, and gives me total control over white balance. You are paying a brand tax when you buy Elgato, but the integration with the Stream Deck and other Elgato products is a convenience I really appreciate.

Elgato Ring Light: Note: It seems Elgato doesn't sell the ring light at the moment, so I've linked to a Neewer equivalent, a brand I have familiarity with, if not the specific product. When it comes time to focus on the small details, this light makes sure everything is evenly lit. It gives me complete control over white balance and intensity, and doubles as a fill light for my talking head footage.

Elgato Stream Deck XL: The Stream Deck XL gives me an easy way to control my stream, and also pulls double duty by giving me access to a huge set of macro features in my productivity applications. It’s massively customizable, and each button is its own little display. I even use it during my games of D&D to set ambience music and play sound effects at key moments.

Elgato HD60 S Capture Card: Note: Another product that has been sundowned. Most recent equivalent linked. I use this Elgato Capture Card to capture footage from my DSLR camera. During streams this is how I show what I’m working on, and during video production it lets me monitor what I’m doing without having to physically be behind the camera. The HD60 S is limited to 1080p60, but its big brother, the HD60 S+ promised 4k60 with HDR10 support.

Elgato Facecam: I use the Elgato Facecam during streams to provide a wider view of my work area and myself. This camera is full 1080 and has relatively good control software. That said, it's on the pricier side, and I don't know that it fully justifies the brand tax.
